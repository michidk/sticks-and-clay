This collection delivers pixel art, drawn cell by cell on a small grid with a fixed palette, never generated as a photograph or painting. Design for that medium and be concrete:
- Grid: the size or sizes (8 for tiny items, 16 for icons, 32 for characters and props, 48 to 64 for large characters and scenes; at most 128), preferably multiples of 8 so the art fits tile grids, one square pixel scale for the whole collection, native resolution as the delivered asset.
- Palette: a named classic palette (Game Boy, PICO-8, DawnBringer 16, Sweetie 16, Endesga 32, NES or 1-bit; a named palette is enforced exactly, so name one only when the collection must stay inside it) or a custom one, as a Markdown table of names and hex values. Budget by subject (props 4 to 6 colors, characters 8 to 12, large creatures 12 to 16, scenes 8 to 16). Build it from ramps of 3 to 5 steps per material plus one or two accents, laid out dark to light in the table: from base to shadow turn the hue about 15 to 20 degrees toward blue or purple and add a little saturation, from base to highlight turn it about 15 to 20 degrees toward yellow or orange, lose a little saturation and raise the value. Use tinted darks and lights instead of pure black and white, and one shared shadow hue and highlight hue.
- Light and shading: one light direction (top-left unless stated), the number of shading steps, cast shadows where forms overlap, no pillow shading, no banding, and whether dithering is allowed (ordered patterns only, and never on small art).
- Outline: one rule for the whole collection (full, selective or none), always one cell wide, in a near-black tint or the darkest shade, never pure black. A selective outline runs once around each figure; forms inside are separated by a darker tone of the front form.
- Silhouette and proportions: bold readable silhouettes that work as a flat single color at native size; for characters the head count and how tall the figure stands on its canvas in cells (for example about 54 to 60 cells on a 64-cell canvas); how the art sits on the grid (ground-standing art centred on one shared baseline, icons centred with an even margin, tiles edge to edge and wrapping); facing; detail only where it carries meaning.
- Animation and layers: only when the brief asks for motion (an idle, walk, flicker) state the frame count (2 to 8), the frames per second and whether it loops, and that the silhouette and baseline stay stable while secondary parts move. Only when it asks for equipment, outfits or swappable parts state paperdoll layers: the base body and the named layers bottom to top, and the attach points (grip, head top, feet). A still collection says neither.
- Variation rules across the set, and exclusions: no anti-aliasing at 16x16 and below, no gradients, blur, semi-transparency, text, sub-pixel detail or mixed pixel sizes.

Always include exactly one section headed `## Pixel palette`. List the complete collection palette there as a Markdown table with color names and six-digit #rrggbb values, 1 to 64 distinct opaque colors. This section is the authoritative palette used by the generator and displayed as swatches above reference images.
